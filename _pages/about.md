Welcome to my website. I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Immigration Policy Lab and the Public Policy Group at ETH Zürich. My research uses quantitative methods to analyse public discourse and public opinion in democracies - how political outsiders can shape discourse through protest, and how online discursive spaces can be designed to promote constructive speech and minimise hateful and harmful content.

I completed my PhD in Political Science at University College London in 2025. My thesis examined how social movements amplify their message in the media and persuade the public of their demands through protest. I focused, in particular, on the trade-off faced by protesters between winning attention through disruptive (and often unpopular) actions versus maximising persuasive appeal through peaceful actions. Using transformer-based NLP, I constructed a new protest event dataset (N=1700) from the social media streams of 85 protest groups active in the UK between 2010-2020 (British Environmental and Economic Protest Event Dataset or 'BEEPED'). With this dataset, I demonstrate the substantial attention premium associated with confrontational protest tactics. I also explore the downstream effects of coverage on the discourse surrounding the protesters' issue.
