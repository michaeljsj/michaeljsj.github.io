excerpt: 'To attract attention, protesters sometimes resort to the use of disruptive or even violent tactics. To what extent do such tactics undermine the persuasiveness of the group’s message? This study uses a factorial vignette survey experiment of 4,087 UK adults to decompose variation in protest tactics into multiple dimensions (legality, scope, disruption, violence) and examine their effects on the perceived acceptability and persuasiveness of protest across three issues and twelve protest demands. Using a quasi-prepost design, I test whether protests are persuasive, ineffective or dissuasive relative to a ‘no protest’ counterfactual. I find that while more extreme tactics dampen persuasive effects, they tend not to result in dissuasion, even for the most extreme tactics analysed (property damage). There is also no evidence of policy belief polarisation, despite a high correlation between ex-ante support and approval of protesters’ tactics. These results speak directly to contemporary debates over strategic choices facing protesters.'
